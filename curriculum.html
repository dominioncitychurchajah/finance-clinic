What makes this a school, not a seminar.

Four things separate a curriculum you finish from a talk you enjoyed.

Both courses, same night

Scripture and arithmetic are never separated. Topic 5 on the tithe and Topic 22 on budgeting sit in the same term, taught by the same people.

Every topic has an assignment

Not reflection questions. A debt list with rates. A written budget. A freedom date. Work that gets reviewed by name.

Nigerian numbers throughout

Naira inflation, treasury bills, domiciliary accounts, rent cycles, black tax. Case studies from Lagos, not Los Angeles.

Paced so you finish

Four classes a month for ninety days per block. Frequent enough to hold momentum, spaced enough to do the homework.

Every class comes with:

A written assignment

Issued in the last ten minutes of every session, in writing, with a due date. This is the accountability engine.

An anchoring story

Every topic carries a story — scriptural or contemporary — so the principle has somewhere to live in your memory.

Small group discussion

Fifteen minutes in groups of four to six. You say your own numbers out loud to people who will remember them.

A recording within 48 hours

Missing a class does not put you behind. The recording and the assignment both come to you.

Printed notes on request

Tick the box at registration and your notes arrive on paper each month. No smartphone required, no explanation needed.

Opening and closing prayer

Ten minutes at each end. The lesson is spoken over your finances before anyone leaves the room.

How the month runs

Spirit first, mind second, then both again.

The alternation is the theology of the programme made into a timetable. You are never asked to choose between obedience and competence, because you are never taught them apart.

  • Week 1 and Week 3 — Kingdom Finance, 90 minutes
